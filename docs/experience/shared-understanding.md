## A blind person and a sighted person plan a repair

Maya reads and enters information through Braille and conversation. Theo prefers
a visual workspace. They want to understand a leaking water heater, agree on a
safe immediate response, and prepare for a plumber.

<div class="journey-scene">
  <section><p class="scene-label">Shared intent</p><h3>Make the area safe and prepare accurate information for qualified help</h3><p>The household space contains the appliance record, manual, shutoff location, prior service, and approved photos. Each person retains private preferences and interaction state.</p></section>
  <section><p class="scene-label">Maya's experience</p><h3>Conversation and structured Braille</h3><p>Dialogue explains the detected leak location and asks before using a recent photo. Braille regions provide the model number, water shutoff steps from the manual, uncertainty, plumber questions, and direct confirmation controls.</p></section>
  <section><p class="scene-label">Theo's experience</p><h3>Visual composition and touch</h3><p>A visual layout shows the appliance image, highlighted shutoff location, cited manual steps, uncertainty, and a shared checklist. Touch controls support direct selection and confirmation.</p></section>
  <section><p class="scene-label">Shared result</p><h3>One decision with equivalent control</h3><p>Both people receive the same safety state, evidence, uncertainty, choices, and confirmation. Either person can pause the plan. The shared receipt records the agreed action while private modality preferences remain personal.</p></section>
</div>

The semantic outcome provides the shared foundation. Maya receives a native
tactile and conversational experience. Theo receives a native visual and touch
experience. Their actions operate on the same governed semantic state.

## A Deaf person and a hearing person coordinate a visit

Amara communicates primarily in ASL. Her father uses speech and has limited sign
fluency. They want to review the agenda for a specialist visit and agree on the
questions they will ask.

- Amara signs her intent. A configured ASL adapter proposes the interpretation
  and presents it visually for review.
- Her experience uses sign output where available, concise text, visual
  structure, and explicit source links.
- Her father's experience uses conversation with a visual summary for exact
  names, dates, and questions.
- The shared space contains the agreed agenda. Each person's health context,
  notes, and disclosure decisions remain independently governed.
- Both participants confirm which questions and records will be shared with the
  clinician.

Recognition confidence and language variation remain visible. The participants
can refine the interpretation before it influences the shared agenda.

## A person changes modality with context

Jordan begins comparing insurance options on a visual display. When it is time
to drive, the active experience shifts to concise conversation. Detailed tables
and citations remain available for later review.

Unison preserves the comparison intent, selected options, source provenance,
unresolved questions, and confirmation state. The driving experience presents
only the information appropriate to that environment. On arrival, Jordan can
resume through visual, Braille, or conversational output from the same semantic
state.

## A future constrained BCI pathway

A person with limited voluntary movement may use a qualified BCI adapter to
express a small set of well-calibrated signals such as select, next, pause, and
request assistance. Conversation, visual output, Braille, or another configured
modality can provide the detailed response.

BCI-derived signals remain evidence of intent. Identity, assurance, policy, and
confirmation determine which operations those signals can authorize. Higher-risk
actions can require an independently configured confirmation route.

## What equivalent participation means

Every participant can:

- understand the shared intent and current state;
- identify sources, uncertainty, and important assumptions;
- perceive privacy and sensing state;
- review choices and consequences;
- confirm, cancel, correct, and recover through their native experience;
- distinguish personal context from shared context; and
- receive a durable outcome in an appropriate form.

Equivalent participation focuses on meaning and agency. The amount, pacing, and
organization of information can vary by modality and person.
